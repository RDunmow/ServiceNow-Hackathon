Building, testing and getting demo-ready

Build in small steps

The most common hackathon mistake is asking for everything at once, then spending day two untangling it. Instead:

  1. Get the core working first. Your "must have" scope from the planning template: main table, form and the one process at the heart of your idea.

  2. Test it. Add sample records and walk through it as a user would.

  3. Add one feature at a time. ServiceNow recommends developing each page or feature in a separate conversation: implement it, test it, then start a new conversation for the next one.

  4. Repeat. Each approved plan creates a checkpoint, so you always have a point to return to.

Test early and often

Don't wait until the end to find out something doesn't work.

  • Create sample records (ask Build Agent to generate them; see the template in Guide 3).
  • Log in or impersonate as each role to check what they can see and do.
  • Work through the test scenarios in section 10 of your planning template, including the awkward cases: missing fields, rejected requests, overlapping dates.

When things go wrong

What's happening What to do
Build Agent changed something you didn't want Open the checkpoints and roll back to the last good one.
It misunderstood what you asked Roll back, then rewrite your prompt with more detail and platform terms.
A page or form looks wrong Attach a screenshot and describe what should change.
The conversation has become long and confused Mark it complete and start a new conversation for the next feature.
You're not sure what it changed Check the conversation change log. It lists every file Build Agent updated.
Stuck for more than 15 minutes Call a mentor.

Finishing a piece of work

When a feature is done, prompt Build Agent to mark the conversation complete. The changes from each checkpoint are packaged into update sets, which you can find on the Deployment tab of the ServiceNow Studio home page. You won't need to deploy anything during the hackathon, but it's useful to know your work is tracked and portable.

Adding AI

When your "must have" scope works and you've tested it, you're ready to add a skill or an AI agent. Follow Guide 5. Treat AI like any other feature: one at a time, tested before you move on, and finished before the demo freeze.

Getting demo-ready

Your demo is how the judges see your work, so protect it.

Demo freeze. At the agreed freeze time, stop building new features. Use the remaining time to test and rehearse. Breaking a working app in the last hour is the most common way demos fail.
