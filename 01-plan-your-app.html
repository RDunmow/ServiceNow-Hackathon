Plan your app

New to building with AI? Start with the AI vibe coding primer.

Build Agent can build an app in minutes. That makes it tempting to start typing straight away. Don't. The teams that do best at hackathons are the ones who know exactly what they're building before they build it.

ServiceNow's own guidance for Build Agent says the same: design before coding, document your requirements across the data and user interface, and agree the scope, tables and record types before you start.

Plan for about 60 to 90 minutes on day 1. It feels like a long time. It saves you far more.

Start with the finished product

Before you talk about tables and fields, picture the end result. Work through these three exercises as a team.

Write the headline

Imagine your app went live tomorrow. Write the one-sentence announcement.

Example

"Anyone can report an office problem in 30 seconds, and it reaches the right team without anyone sorting an inbox."

If you can't agree on the headline, you haven't agreed on the problem yet. Go back and talk it through.

Tell the demo story

At the end of day 2 you'll have about 5 minutes to show your app. Write that demo now, step by step, from the user's point of view.

Example
  1. Alex notices the heating isn't working in meeting room 3B and reports it in a couple of sentences.
  2. The issue is categorised as Heating and cooling, marked High priority, and routed to the Building services team. Two similar reports from the same floor are flagged as possible duplicates.
  3. Jordan in Building services picks it up, fixes it, and resolves it with a note.
  4. Alex gets a notification that it's fixed.

Your demo story becomes your test plan, your scope, and your pitch. Everything you build should serve it.

Sketch the screens

Grab paper and draw what each person sees: the form they fill in, the list they work from, the page they land on. It doesn't need to be neat. Take a photo; you can share sketches and screenshots with Build Agent later.

Use the planning template

Copy the team planning template into a shared document and fill it in together. Here's what each section is for and the questions to ask yourselves.

Section Why it matters Ask yourselves
1. The problem Judges score business value. A sharp problem makes a strong pitch. Who feels this pain? What does it cost today?
2. The people Every role becomes a set of permissions and screens. Who uses it, and what do they each need to do?
3. The finished product Keeps the team aligned on one outcome. What's the headline? What's the demo story?
4. Scope Stops you running out of time. What must work by end of day 1? What can wait?
5. Data Tables and fields are the foundation of your app, and the hardest thing to change later. What information do we store? What can we reuse from the platform?
6. The process Becomes your states, approvals and notifications. What starts it? What happens next? Who needs to know?
7. Rules and logic Makes the app smart, not just a form. What must the app prevent or calculate?
8. Where people work Shapes the user interface Build Agent creates. Where does each person spend their time?
9. Who can see and do what Becomes your roles and access controls. Who can create, read, update or delete each record?
10. Test scenarios Proves the app works, including when things go wrong. What's the happy path? What's the awkward case?
11. How you'll show value Turns a demo into a business case. What would we measure if this went live?
12. Assumptions and risks Shows the judges you've thought it through. What are we assuming? What don't we know?
13. Where could AI help? Identifies where a skill or an AI agent adds real value (see Guide 5). Where do people read, sort or judge things today?

Tips for planning well

  • Make the "must have" list small. One table, one process, one happy path. You can always add more on day 2.
  • Reuse what's already there. The platform already knows about users, groups, locations and departments. Reference them rather than building your own.
  • Name things properly. Decide your table and field names now. Clear names make clearer prompts.
  • Write rules as sentences. "Never allow an issue to be resolved without resolution notes" is easy to turn into a prompt.
  • Build the app before the AI. A skill or agent needs a working app, real fields and sample data to work with. Plan for AI, but build it once your "must have" scope works.
  • Use made-up data. Plan your sample records with fictional names and values. No real customer, personal or sensitive data.
